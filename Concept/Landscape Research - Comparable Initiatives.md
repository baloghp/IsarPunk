# Landscape Research: Comparable Initiatives to IsarPunk

*Research note for seed planning · August 2026 (updated for ESU 2027 + wide-workshop strategy) · Sources linked at end*

IsarPunk sits at the intersection of three existing movements: **school Linux / FOSS clubs**, **repair & circular-electronics education**, and **civic youth tech communities** (especially Germany’s Chaos / Jugend hackt ecosystem). This article maps the closest comparable initiatives — what they deliver, what appeals, what worked, and what IsarPunk can steal without reinventing.

**IsarPunk strategy note (current):** consumer Windows 10 ESU now runs to **12 October 2027**, giving more runway. Primary goal is **~6 school workshops over the next school year** — **wide knowledge dispersal and seed-planting**, not guiding one cohort through a full solution. Deep installs / refurb belong to **MuCCC and other existing labs**; IsarPunk’s job is awareness + warm handoff.

---

## 1. Why this landscape matters

Windows 10’s consumer Extended Security Updates cliff (**October 2027**, after an extension from the earlier 2026 plan) remains a time-boxed catalyst, not a unique idea. The extra year is an advantage: sow widely while machines are still patchable, then hand motivated youth and families to hubs before the final cliff.

Peers have already proven that **kids + discarded hardware + open software + a real beneficiary** produces durable outcomes. For IsarPunk’s *dispersal* mode, the closer analogues are **Chaos macht Schule / Restart session packs / Schule & Co** (many short touches) more than a single Penguin-style deep club — though Penguin/Restart still teach messaging, cards, and partner design.

The pattern that repeats across continents:

> Learn the tool → serve someone with it → make the service public → keep a hangout so people return.

**IsarPunk variant for year one:**

> Spark the story in many classrooms → leave a next-step path to MuCCC / repair labs → measure workshops run and referrals made, not machines finished.

## 3. Deep profiles

### 3.1 Asian Penguins & Penguin Corps (Stu Keroff) — closest FOSS-school cousin

**What it is.** Middle-school Linux clubs (first widely documented school LUG of its kind). Kids don’t only *use* Linux — they install, configure, and apply it.

**Three activity pillars** (from the *Linux Club Guide*):

1. Kids learn Linux (install & configure)
2. Kids use Linux to help their schools
3. Kids use Linux to help communities

**Appeals.** Identity (“Penguins”), peer status, and a mission families understand: *your child brought a computer to a family that needed one*. Immigrant and low-income school contexts made the digital-divide story visceral, not abstract.

**Outcomes & impacts.**

- Asian Penguins: **200+** alumni over ~7 years; **241** Linux PCs given away by ~2019 (~1/week cadence); wait list for needy students driven down to days/weeks; school digital divide declared closed at one point.
- Penguin Corps (Aspen Academy): pre-COVID gifts small; then **60** machines in ~7 weeks of lockdown — principal credited the club with enabling distance learning for all families.
- Soft impact repeatedly reported: shy students become mission leaders; special-needs students get visible roles; kids present at conferences (DrupalCon, state education webinars, MPR/TV coverage).

**Key deliverables.** Refurbished PCs; home “Penguin Missions”; classroom Linux fleets; the freely published **Linux Club Guide** as replication kit.

**Pivotal success factors.**

- Teacher embedded in school (policy back door via extracurricular, not “replace Windows school-wide”)
- Hardware pipeline (Free Geek grants, community donations — publicity → more donations)
- Service climax (delivery day with translation/support), not a certificate ceremony
- Start tiny; grow the mission as capacity appears

**IsarPunk takeaway.** “Save Oma” is the Munich equivalent of a Penguin Mission — same emotional arc, different beneficiary story (security/obsolescence vs. access).

### 3.2 Restart Project — Restart at School & intensive labs (UK)

**What it is.** NGO-backed **10-week enrichment** for secondary students on design, science, ethics, and social impact of repair, co-supported by volunteer Restarters. Climax: a **Restart Party** open to the community.

**Appeals.** Dual audience by design: sustainability kids who don’t identify as “technical,” and technical kids who need social/communication skills. Gender balance improved when framed as *making gadgets last*, not as geek club.

**Session arc (illustrative).** E-waste & worker stress → safe teardown → organised repair research → upgradability → batteries/minerals → practice on headphones/controllers → design critique → reuse value → circular careers → public party.

**Outcomes & impacts.**

- Transferable pack of teacher materials (Restart’s strategy: **enable teachers**, not become a permanent school delivery org)
- Documented student excitement and first-time screwdriver competence on school laptop screens
- **Laptop Ninja** summer school (asylum-seeking youth): students upgraded donated laptops (SSD/RAM), dual-booted, took machines home; **72%** reported increased confidence with computers or repair generally

**Key deliverables.** Curriculum sessions, repair parties, upgraded student-owned machines, confidence survey evidence.

**IsarPunk takeaway.** End every pilot cohort with a public “Oma / family install & repair afternoon.” Publish workshop cards so *teachers* can rerun without you.

### 3.3 Riot Refurb (South Portland High School, Maine)

**What it is.** Classroom + after-school **device repair lab** scavenging unused school Chromebooks/iPads/MacBooks. Devices sold cheaply or donated; revenue buys parts.

**Appeals.** Career-adjacent skills (diagnosis, process, patience) + visible community help (“4 kids, this helps my family”).

**Outcomes.** **200+** devices repaired (reported ~2023); open **Riot Refurb 2.0 guidebook** covering curriculum, e-waste context, parts, inclusion, ethics, outreach.

**IsarPunk takeaway.** School closets are a hardware source. Partner with school IT for “dead” fleet machines; keep a simple money loop (stickers/parts), not a charity black hole.

---

### 3.4 Free Geek (Portland OR & Twin Cities)

**What it is.** Mature nonprofit hub: intake → responsible recycle → Linux (or Windows) rebuild → thrift / free programs → classes (basics, soldering, Linux, security).

**Appeals.** Low-barrier **$35 Linux desktop bundles**; volunteer identity; civic “digital divide” framing at city scale.

**Outcomes (Twin Cities, 2024 reporting).** ~**95 tons** recycled; **43,000+** refurbished pieces rehomed; education catalog alongside storefront.

**IsarPunk takeaway.** You don’t need to *be* Free Geek. You need a **hub relationship** (Munich: shops, FabLab, MuCCC) that supplies machines and absorbs toxic/hard cases while kids do software & soft missions.

---

### 3.5 Labdoo (global; strong DE/CH presence)

**What it is.** Volunteer **hub network** that sanitises donated laptops, loads educational software, and ships via “Dootrips” (luggage space — CO₂-light narrative). Recipients: schools, orphanages, refugee projects (“Edoovillages”).

**Appeals.** Crystal-clear ask: *donate a laptop, spread education*. Platform coordinates hubs, travel, and school demand.

**Outcomes (org-reported).** On the order of **tens of thousands** of laptops, **thousands** of educational projects, **hundred-thousands to ~1M** learners reached; DE hubs also addressed home-schooling device gaps citing Bitkom’s discarded-PC stock.

**IsarPunk takeaway.** Labdoo proves **platform + hubs** scales donation logistics. For a 3-person Munich seed, borrow the *clarity of ask*, not the global logistics stack — local Oma installs beat international shipping year one.

---

### 3.6 Jugend hackt (OKF / mediale pfade)

**What it is.** Ages ~12–18; motto *Mit Code die Welt verbessern*. Weekend events + **Labs** (year-round local offers) + online community + youth advisory board. Mentors co-build civic tech projects.

**Appeals.** Agency and politics of tech without pure vocational coding bootcamp energy; diversity Code of Conduct as product feature; alumni become mentors/workshop leads.

**Outcomes (2024).** ~**350** weekend participants, **53** projects, **150** mentors; **20** Labs with **400+** offers reaching **3,000+** youth; Munich already hosts events (culture partners like MPZ).

**IsarPunk takeaway.** Don’t compete — **route advanced kids** into Jugend hackt Labs/events. Use IsarPunk for the Windows-EOL / repair / family-mission niche they don’t fully own. Co-mentor swaps beat parallel infrastructure.

---

### 3.7 Chaos macht Schule (CCC)

**What it is.** Since ~2007, Erfa-Kreis outreach to schools/parents/teachers: privacy, social networks, copyright, Medienkompetenz; sometimes Medienscouts; Munich history of teacher further-education with universities.

**Appeals.** Credibility of CCC; “understand systems so you aren’t owned by them” — culturally adjacent to IsarPunk’s *take control back*.

**Outcomes.** Durable volunteer education brand; Youth Hacking Day at Congress; local school relationships that IsarPunk can tap via MuCCC rather than cold-calling Rektorate.

**IsarPunk takeaway.** Borrow Cms workshop culture (short, shocking demos, teacher-usable tools). Frame Linux install as *Medienkompetenz + Sicherheit*, which schools already budget attention for.

---

### 3.8 German repair-education research & networks

**RETIBNE** (Oldenburg-led, DBU-funded) built Technik/Informatik materials on repair and lifetime extension, with multi-university and school partners.

**Classroom repair-café studies** (reported via Raspberry Pi Foundation / Hello World): three German schools; smartphone stations (cables / displays / upcycle); mentors ask *“Why isn’t this working?”*; students distributed evenly across stations — upcycling valued equally with fix.

**Netzwerk Reparatur-Initiativen – Schule & Co** catalogues school–café cooperation patterns and handouts.

**IsarPunk takeaway.** Station rotation + mentor questions is a battle-tested workshop format for Projektwoche. Upcycle success criteria prevent demoralisation when a laptop won’t revive.

## 4. Cross-cutting success factors

What the landscape converges on:

| Factor | Evidence across peers |
|---|---|
| **Mission over tooling** | Penguins, Restart, Labdoo, Riot — Linux/repair is the *means* |
| **Public climax** | Restart Party, Penguin Mission, Jugend hackt demo day |
| **Adult gatekeeper inside institution** | Teacher (Keroff, York, Archer Academy DT lead) |
| **Hardware & liability outsourced to hubs** | Free Geek, shops, repair cafés, FabLabs |
| **Replication kit > custom platform** | Linux Club Guide, Restart packs, Riot Refurb 2.0 |
| **Peer mentoring ladder** | Jugend hackt alumni → mentors; shy kids → mission leads |
| **Tight metric for the story** | Devices given, confidence %, school divide closed, projects shipped |
| **Back door into schools** | AG / enrichment / Projektwoche before IT policy wars |

What rarely appears as year-one success: a bespoke youth social network. Engagement layers (Discord/Matrix/forum) work *after* events create membership.

IsarPunk’s deck already stacks several hooks (*Save Oma*, e-waste tower, Gen Alpha builders). Peers show you should **pick one primary hook per event**, not all at once.

## 6. Deliverables inventory (steal-ready)

Replication artefacts peers publish that IsarPunk can mirror at seed scale:

1. **Workshop cards** (90-min blocks with aims, materials, safety) — Restart / Cms style  
2. **USB / image checklist** — Penguin / Free Geek style  
3. **Family mission script** (“Oma talk” + 1-page cheat sheet) — Penguin Mission analogue  
4. **Partner one-pager** (what we ask / what you get) — Free Geek / shop ally  
5. **Consent & photo Elternbrief** — Jugend hackt / school norm  
6. **Public event recipe** (Restart Party / Save Oma day)  
7. **Short impact log** (devices, kids, partners, quotes) for the next €  

Optional later: Labdoo-like intake form; Riot-like parts ledger; Jugend hackt-like Lab calendar.

## 7. Positioning IsarPunk in this field

| Gap peers leave open in Munich / 2026 | IsarPunk fit |
|---|---|
| Win10 ESU cliff as urgent local narrative | Time-boxed campaign energy |
| Intergenerational “Save Oma” service | Stronger than pure digital-divide US framing for DE middle class + seniors |
| Bridge between schools, CCC, and *commercial* repair shops | Deck already lists Munich shops + MuCCC + FabLab |
| Lightweight FOSS sovereignty (Pi-hole, Nextcloud, Jellyfin) for teens | Beyond “install Mint and stop” |

IsarPunk should not try to out-scale Jugend hackt or out-logistics Labdoo. It should own the **Munich EOL awareness → school dispersal → warm handoff** corridor and treat MuCCC / repair cafés / FabLab as the *solution layer*, not competitors.

---

## 8. Implications for a €1,000 / 3-volunteer seed

Given **wide dispersal** (~6 workshops / school year) and the **2027 ESU runway**, a credible seed delivers:

- **~6** school workshops (same core format, many rooms — not one deep cohort)  
- **Replication pack v0** (90-min cards + Elternbrief + “where to go next” partner sheet)  
- **Documented handoff** to MuCCC / repair shops / FabLab / Repair Café (flyer, visit slot, or mentor invite)  
- Optional: **1** joint awareness day *hosted by a partner hub* (IsarPunk brings the story; hub owns tools/liability)  
- Lightweight list/hangout only if it serves booking the next workshop — **not** a custom platform  

Success metrics shift from “machines finished by us” to **classrooms reached, take-home materials left, and successful referrals into existing labs**.

Closest peer modes for this shape: **Chaos macht Schule** (many short school touches), **Restart session cards** (teacher-reusable pack), **Schule & Co** (school ↔ repair-café bridge). Deep Penguin/Riot club models remain inspiration for *partners*, not for IsarPunk’s year-one KPI.
