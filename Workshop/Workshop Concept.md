## 1. Where this format comes from

Derived from **Climate Fresk** pedagogy (Peter is trained), not from a slide deck. A full Fresk wants ~3 hours; the *Fresque du Numérique* junior version runs ~2. This is a Fresk-shaped **88 minutes**, not a shortened Fresk.

What we keep from Fresk: cards dealt in lots, the room discovers rather than receives, a creative/ownership phase, then action.
What we drop: 42 cards, a closed cause-effect chain, three hours, and an emotions round.

The Win10 → Linux story is a **decision tree**, not a physics cascade. So the cards branch — and they branch toward **named endings** (§5.7).

---

## 2. Design principles

| Principle | Consequence in the room |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Belief first** | No facts before we harvest what kids already think |
| **Discovery over lecture** | Myth-busts are *found* on a real machine, not asserted by the facilitator |
| **Agency over doom** | Bad news never stands alone — it is always adjacent to an action block |
| **No public wrong answers** | Continuum placement, not true/false scoring — confidence is the KPI |
| **Honest caveats** | We name what does *not* work (anti-cheat games, Office macros); a kid who fails at home is a kid we lost |
| **Actions have endings** | Every mission board resolves into a named outcome — including the outcome where nothing happens |
| **Partners are named when earned** | No partner monologue. We name the shop the moment a team's own arrow needs one (§5.8) |

## 3. Format at a glance

| | |
|---|---|

| **Group** | ~12 students (works 8–16) |

| **Facilitators** | **2 required** — the two stations run *in parallel*, one adult each |
| **Machines** | **2 laptops**, both genuinely old (pre-2018) |

| **Network** | **Assume none** — everything preloaded offline |
| **Room** | Tables for teams of 3–4 · wall/floor space for the continuum · power at both station tables |

## 4. The 88 minutes

| Min | Block | Mode | Cards |
|-----|-------|------|-------|
| 0–3 | Confidence **in** | Floor continuum, photographed | Set E |
| 3–6 | Hook | Facilitator, short | — |
| 6–12 | **Primer: layer sort** | Teams place logo cards | Set A |
| 12–24 | **Lot 1: opinions** | Teams place on continuum | Set B (all 10) |
| 24–26 | **The flip: you already use Linux** | Whole room, **no machines** | — |
| 26–44 | **Two stations, in parallel** · swap at 35 | Hands on keyboards | Sheets F1 / F2 |
| 44–56 | **Mission 1: the Win10 news** | Actions + 3 endings + dots | Sets C, O |
| 56–80 | **Mission 2: household e-waste** | Actions + all endings + dots · **shops named in-flight** | Sets D, O |
| 80–88 | Close: take-home + confidence **out** | Continuum, mission line, partner list | Set E |

**Never sacrifice:** the stations and Mission 2. Mission 1 is deliberately the small one — its job is to teach the two-tier mechanic so Mission 2 needs no instructions.

### Where the buffer goes

In a 2-hour slot, ~32 minutes sit outside the 88. That is not spare — it is where sessions actually leak:

| Buffer | Realistic cost |
|--------|----------------|
| Arrival, settling, coats, "which room is it" | 5–10 min |
| Lot 1 overrunning — the most elastic block | up to 5 min |
| Mission 2 overrunning — the second most elastic | up to 5 min |
| A USB boot failing and needing a second stick | 2–5 min |
| Packing up, boards taped to the wall, goodbyes | 5–8 min |

If none of that happens, run the expansions in §9 rather than finishing early.

## 5. Block-by-block

### 5.1 Confidence in (0–3)

Five statements on the wall (Set E), three zones on the floor: **JA / UNSICHER / NEIN**. Students stand in a zone per statement. Facilitator photographs the distribution.

**Anonymous aggregate only** — no names, no sheets collected. Keeps schools comfortable and matches the minimal-data stance in the [platform concept](../Engagement%20Platform/High-Level%20Concept.md).

### 5.2 Hook (3–6)

Who we are (IsarPunk, a Circular Munich e.V. project), and explicitly what we are **not**: we do not repair your devices, and we are not here to make anyone feel guilty.

Do **not** explain Linux yet. Do not use the word *Betriebssystem* as if it is understood.

### 5.3 Primer — layer sort (6–12)

Three labelled rows on each table: **GERÄT · BETRIEBSSYSTEM · APP**. Teams place logo cards (Set A).

Purpose: kids cannot hold an opinion about operating systems until the word means something. A definition slide would break the belief-first principle, so the primer is a sort, not an explanation.

Facilitator: circulate, ask *"warum habt ihr die da hingelegt?"*, correct nothing yet. Leave the **Android** card visible on the OS row — it is the setup for §5.5.

### 5.4 Lot 1 — opinion cards (12–24)

Teams of 3–4 place Set B on **STIMMT / UNSICHER / STIMMT NICHT**. All ten cards, dealt in two lots of five.

**No answers are given in this block.** Resist every urge. The cards are revisited at the close (§5.9) after the machines have answered them.

Facilitator listens for which misconceptions are strongest — that is the material the stations have to dismantle.

**This is the most elastic block in the session.** If it runs long, take it from the buffer, not from Mission 2.

### 5.5 The flip — you already use Linux (24–26)

Two minutes, whole room, **no machines needed** — which is why it survives even when the stations run in parallel.

Call back Lot 1: *"Hände hoch, wer bei 'Ich habe Linux noch nie benutzt' auf STIMMT gelegt hat."* Then the Android card still sitting on the OS row from the primer, and the phones in their pockets. Android runs a Linux kernel. The Steam Deck is Linux. Steam Deck also links back to card A15.

They are not beginners. That is the confidence lever, and it costs two minutes and no hardware.

Then split the room and send each half to a station.

### 5.6 Two stations, in parallel (26–44)

Two groups of ~6, **both running at once**, one facilitator each. Swap at ~35. Everyone holds a printed sheet at both stations and ticks along.

| Station | Machine | Sheet | Job |
|---------|---------|-------|-----|
| **A — Flavours** | Old hero machine, booted from USB sticks | F1 | Distros exist, they're free, nothing gets installed |
| **B — Treasure hunt** | Second machine, installed Linux, fully loaded | F2 | Find the software (the myth-busts) |

**Station A is a distro tasting, not a single boot.** Boot **Linux Mint** (friendly, familiar layout), then reboot live into **Bazzite** (gaming-focused, Steam built in, the SteamOS family). Two different desktops on the same old laptop, from two sticks, with nothing installed on the machine.

- The boot wait **is** content, not dead air: *"right now this computer has no operating system at all — in about a minute it will have one."*
- Whichever distro is up when a group arrives, boot the other one in front of them. Order doesn't matter, so the swap needs no special handling.
- **Let students hold a stick.** "This is an entire operating system, it costs nothing, and it fits in your pocket" is a better line when it's in their hand.
- Budget realistically: USB 2.0 on a 2015 machine can take 1–2 minutes per boot. Two boots in a 9-minute half works if one distro is already up.

**Station B rules:**
- **Rotate the keyboard** — a different student finds each item
- **Find and tick only.** One thing gets launched, at the end, or you lose ten minutes to Minecraft
- Facilitator states the honest caveats *when the item is found*, not before (see F2 notes)

The Win10 news has **not** been broken yet at this point. Both stations are pure "look what this old thing can do."

### 5.7 Mission 1 — break the Win10 news (44–56)

Now the news, on the back of eighteen minutes of optimism:

- Mainstream Windows 10 support ended in 2025
- Consumer ESU runs to **12 October 2027**
- Roughly **28%** of Windows desktops are still on Windows 10 (StatCounter, mid-2026 — say **"roughly"** and name the source; it measures web traffic, not installed machines)
- Those are mostly people like Oma, who never heard the word "cliff"

**This block is two-tier.** One shared A2 for the whole room:

1. **Lay out the three endings first** (Set O, subset O1–O3) along the right-hand edge: *it runs Linux and gets used* · *the shop sorted it* · *nothing happened and it's unsafe*.
2. **Deal the action cards** (Set C, 11) — including the self-teaching routes: ask an AI, find a YouTube tutorial, ask someone who knows, and back up the photos first.
3. **Draw arrows** from actions toward endings. Which actions actually reach an ending? Which chain is shortest?
4. **Dots**: one per student, on the **action they personally will do**.
5. **The payoff** (1 min): read the board back. *"Das sind unsere Punkte. Wo landet Omas PC also?"*

If no chain of dots reaches O1 or O2, then **O3 is the ending** — the PC stays online and unsafe. That is the stakes, discovered rather than preached. Do not soften it and do not lecture about it; just read what the board says.

One shared board here is deliberate: everyone watches the mechanic demonstrated once, in miniature, before Mission 2 scales it up.

### 5.8 Mission 2 — household e-waste (56–80)

The big one, and the block that produces referrals. Same two-tier mechanic, now **one A2 board per team** of 3–4.

1. **Frame the problem** (2 min): the devices already in their own homes. Use the props and the project's own figures — a laptop is roughly **2 kg**, a desktop roughly **8 kg** of e-waste.
2. **Lay out the full ending set** (Set O, O1–O8) down one side of each board — including *it's still in the cupboard* (O7) and *someone who had no computer now has one* (O8).
3. **Deal the action cards** (Set D, ~15). Teams **draw arrows** from actions toward endings: what has to happen before what, which endings need an adult, which need a shop, which path keeps the most material out of the bin.
4. **Name the shops in-flight** — see below.
5. **Dots** (last 5 min): one dot each, on their own team's board, on the action they personally will do.
6. **Gallery walk** (2 min): teams compare which endings their dots actually reach. No presentations — there is no time and they add little.

#### Naming the partners here, not in a separate block

There is **no standalone partner-list section**. Instead, while circulating during the arrow phase, whenever a team's chain reaches the repair card (**D8**) or ending **O2**, name a real shop to that team on the spot:

> *"Das hier braucht eine Adresse. Es gibt einen Laden in …"*

Why this beats a monologue at the end: it lands per team, in the ten seconds after they worked out for themselves that they need a shop, and it costs no session time. A five-minute partner presentation at minute 78 is the worst attention slot in the room.

The **full list goes home in the take-home package** (§5.9), so nobody has to memorise anything.

✅ **Unblocked.** Two Munich repair shops have signed [listing consent](../Artifacts/Partner%20Listing%20Consent.md) (Sep 2026), with more expected. Both facilitators need the shop names and rough locations memorised or on a crib card — see §8.

The endings are what make this block unique to each room. Twelve students produce four different maps and four different answers to *what happens to the laptop in our cupboard*.

### 5.9 Close — take-home and confidence out (80–88)

1. **Hand out the take-home package**, which carries the **named partner list** plus Circular Munich's [CircularCity Map](https://circular-munich.com/circularcitymap/). One sentence, not a presentation: *"alle Läden stehen hier drauf."*
2. Blank line on the sheet: *meine Mission: ______*, filled in from their own dot
3. Revisit two or three Lot 1 cards — *"wer würde seine Karte jetzt verschieben?"*
4. Confidence continuum again (Set E), photographed
5. The Mission 1 board and the team boards **stay with the teacher, on the wall**

## 6. The dot mechanic

**A dot is a personal pledge, not a vote.** *"Das mache ich."*

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Dots go on action cards only** | You can pledge an action; you cannot pledge an outcome. Endings are consequences |
| Decide privately first, place on a count of three | Otherwise the first three dots decide the room |
| One dot per student per mission block | Forces a choice |
| Two dot colours (Mission 1 / Mission 2) | Countable separately in the photo |
| Mission 1 = one shared board · Mission 2 = one board per team | Learn the mechanic together, then own your team's map |
| Read the board back before moving on | The dots plus the arrows *are* the answer to "what happens next" |

**Say the framing explicitly.** "Create a mission" and "pledge individually" sound like the same instruction and are not. The mission is the whole board; the dot is your part of it.

**Nothing is printed twice.** The student's copy is the blank line on the take-home sheet, in their own hand.

**The null endings do real work.** O3 (*nothing happened, it's unsafe*) and O7 (*still in the cupboard*) let the room see the cost of inaction without a facilitator moralising. If the dots don't reach a good ending, say so flatly and move on.

**What it gives us:** photograph the boards, count dots per card ID, and record which **endings** the dots actually reached. Across six workshops that tells us which actions students choose *and* which outcomes the format actually produces — exactly the taxonomy the [engagement platform](../Engagement%20Platform/High-Level%20Concept.md) would need if it is ever un-parked.

**What it does not give us:** proof anyone did it. While the platform is parked, the boards on the classroom wall are our **only** follow-through mechanism — a teacher who can point at them next week. Brief the teacher on this.

## 7. Card sets & worksheets

**Full card text, IDs, DE translations, facilitator notes, and print spec:** [`Card Deck v0.md`](Card%20Deck%20v0.md) — that file is the single source of truth. **Do not duplicate card text here.**

| Set | Name | Cards | Board / mode |
|-----|------|-------|--------------|
| **A** | Layer sort (primer) | 15 (12 core + 3 optional) | Three rows, one set per team of 3 |
| **B** | Opinion cards | 10 | Continuum, one set per team |
| **C** | Mission 1 actions (Win10) | 11 | **One shared A2** for the whole room |
| **D** | Mission 2 actions (e-waste) | ~15 | **One A2 per team** |
| **O** | **Ending cards** — shared by both missions | 8 (Mission 1 uses O1–O3) | Down one edge of every board |
| **E** | Confidence statements | 5, A4 on the wall | Floor zones, whole room |

Set O being **one set used at two scales** is deliberate: Mission 2 revisits endings the room already met in Mission 1, then extends them.

The station sheets below are worksheets rather than cards, so they live in this doc.

### Sheet F1 — Station A (distro flavours)

- What is different between these two desktops? Name one thing each
- Find the software for playing games on **Bazzite**
- Find out how old this laptop is and what's inside it (`fastfetch`)
- Is anything being installed on this computer right now? *(No — it's all running from the stick)*
- What did the operating system on this stick cost? *(Nothing)*

**Facilitator notes:** Mint is the friendly, familiar-looking one. Bazzite is the gaming one with Steam built in and is in the same family as the Steam Deck's OS — link it straight back to the flip in §5.5. Let students hold a stick.

### Sheet F2 — Station B (treasure hunt)

- Something to play games with — and one game that actually launches
- Something to write a school report with
- Something to edit video
- Something to draw or edit photos
- Something to play music or watch a film
- How old is this laptop, and what did it cost new?
- Something you already use every day
- Find out what all this software costs *(nothing)*

**Facilitator honesty notes, stated when found:**
- **Games:** most Steam titles work via Proton, and the Steam Deck is Linux — *but* some competitive games with anti-cheat still do not. Say this out loud.
- **Office:** LibreOffice, or Office in the browser. Heavy macro spreadsheets are the exception.

`fastfetch` doubles as the install-proof ritual described in the platform concept — one habit, not two.

## 9. Fallbacks and expansions

| If | Then |
|----|------|
| **The 2 hours are running clean** | Expand in this order: a third distro at Station A → a real gallery walk with one sentence per team → let Mission 2 run to 28 min → more Lot 1 discussion |
| **School only offers 90 min** | Runnable, tight: Lot 1 → 8 cards (drop B3, B7), primer → 5 min, Mission 2 → 20 min. Everything else stands |
| **School only offers 45–60 min** | Decline, or split across two sessions. Do not cut Mission 2 — it is the referral engine |
| **Only one facilitator** | Stations cannot run in parallel. Run Station B as a guided whole-room hunt with rotating drivers, and cut Station A to a single Mint boot during the Mission 1 framing |
| **Only one laptop** | Station A's distro tasting *is* the session's hands-on beat — keep it, drop Station B and cover the myth-busts verbally from the F2 list. Materially weaker |
| **Slow USB boots** | Pre-boot one distro before the students arrive; only one live boot per group |
| **No partner near this school** | Name the nearest signed shop anyway plus the CircularCity Map, and say plainly it's a tram ride. Better than an unreachable O2 |
| **No network** | This is the assumption, not the exception. Everything preloaded |
| **Deck not printed by workshop #1** | Fall back to the Chaos-macht-Schule shape: short demo + Q&A + verbal missions and a paper take-home. Log the slip in the workstream |

1. **Age band** — 12–15 or 15–18? Set B and the primer are pitched at the younger end (parked since goal-setting)

## 12. What this format is measured on

| Signal | How |
|--------|-----|
| **Confidence pre/post** (lead metric, D5) | Set E continuum, counted from photos |
| Misconception shift | Lot 1 cards revisited at close |
| Which actions students choose | Dot counts per card ID, across six workshops |
| **Which endings the dots reach** | Recorded per board — the format's own output |
| Referrals | Partner survey: *"did anyone mention IsarPunk?"* |
| Format is reusable | A teacher could rerun it from this doc + the deck (train-the-trainer, after #3) |

⚠️ **Watch E5** (*"I know where in Munich you can take a device for repair"*). With no standalone partner block, E5 now depends entirely on shops being named in-flight during Mission 2 plus the take-home package. **If E5 doesn't move across the first two workshops, the in-flight naming isn't working** and the partner beat needs to come back as its own block.
